A Plain-English Primer on AI Governance Frameworks

Every time I bring up "AI governance" outside a classroom, I get the same reaction: eyes glaze over before I finish the sentence. That's a shame, because the actual ideas are simple once you strip the acronyms away.

The EU AI Act sorts AI systems into risk tiers — unacceptable, high, limited, and minimal — and scales obligations to match. A resume-screening tool, for instance, lands in the "high-risk" bucket because it affects someone's access to employment, which means it comes with documentation, human-oversight, and bias-testing requirements attached.

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework takes a less prescriptive approach: instead of fixed tiers, it asks teams to continuously Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage risk throughout a system's lifecycle — closer to a process discipline than a legal checklist.

You don't need a law degree to reason about AI governance — you need to ask "who does this system affect, and how would we know if it was wrong?"

That question is exactly what I tried to build into my resume-screener project: not just "does the model work," but "who gets hurt when it doesn't."
