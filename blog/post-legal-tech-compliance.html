Where Legal Studies Meets Systems Analysis

Halfway through a requirements-gathering interview for my course-registration redesign project, I realized I was doing something I'd practiced before — just not in an MIS class.

Statutory interpretation is the exercise of taking ambiguous, sometimes contradictory language and working out what behavior it actually mandates. Requirements engineering is the same exercise, aimed at stakeholders instead of legislatures: someone says "the system should be fair to all students," and your job is to turn that into something a database schema can enforce.

Ambiguity doesn't go away when you write code — it just moves from the sentence to the edge case.

My Legal Studies certificate didn't teach me SQL, but it did teach me to distrust a requirement until I'd found the edge case that breaks it — the transfer student, the waitlist tie, the prerequisite exception. That habit has made me a noticeably better systems analyst, not a worse one.

If there's a thread running through my coursework, it's this: the interesting problems in MIS aren't technical, they're interpretive. The technology just makes the interpretation load-bearing.
